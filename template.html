User Interviews
Goal
  • Understand what makes each segment choose a service.
  • Identify each segment's behaviors and pain points with other apps.
  • Decide on our main segment and a value proposition.
Participants
4 heavy travelers · 4 FX traders
1Hypotheses from desk research
  • Both segments likely choose services based on exchange fees and rates.
  • Travelers likely value card benefits and exchange currency mainly for traveling. (Koreans travel abroad 1.5x/year)
  • FX traders likely value convenience and information access, and likely exchange currency both for trading and for travel. (Koreans trade FX 5x/day)
2Findings
1 · Exchange fee matters most
100% of users care about the fee and will switch apps for a lower one. Users compare rates across apps, and even within a single app, because buy and sell prices differ.
"I switched banks for '100% fee-free,' then found a fee when I sold."
"Signed up from a 'no fee' ad. Turned out it was USD only, and only that week."
Takeaway: fee is the top reason users choose one service over another.
2 · Users check the daily change first, then the longer-term trend
To find the best rate, both groups first check how much today's rate moved against yesterday, then look at the graph to confirm.
"When I buy, I usually just check how much it dropped today. The graph won't tell me what happens tomorrow anyway."
"Before buying I check the daily, weekly, and yearly graph. When I sell, I just glance at today's change and sell fast."
"I want to buy currency regularly, like stocks, but my app doesn't show the average price or the trend, which is frustrating."
Takeaway: when buying or selling, users decide on the exchange after a quick check of the daily change, not deeper data.
3 · Usage frequency differs by segment
FX traders check the app several times a day, watching the trend graph before buying and the day's return before selling. They get news elsewhere and use the app mainly to trade.
"Before buying, I watch the graph for timing. After buying, I check the rate a few times a day."
Travelers focus on debit-card spend. They exchange before a trip and top up when they run short.
"Before a trip, I check the app every once in a while to see how much the rate's dropped that day, and if it's dropped enough, I exchange a big chunk at once. I don't really open the app much when I'm actually traveling."
Takeaway: users watch the rate most closely right before exchanging. Travelers' engagement drops sharply afterward.
4 · Competitor apps are hard to understand
Users don't get the jargon or the complex structure.
"The first time I used the app I had to Google what it meant."
"A friend recommended a cheap app, but I couldn't find where to exchange, so I gave up."
Takeaway: simpler IA and plain language are a clear opportunity.

Competitor Analysis

I analyzed how existing products met the needs from our interviews, where they fell short, and where we could differentiate.

Goal
  • Examine how competitors display buy / sell fees, and whether the two prices actually differ.
  • See how they show the rate trend.
  • Check whether they actually use hard-to-understand terms.
  • Review their signup and exchange flows.
  • Spot any other distinctive points.
Apps researched
Travel apps
  • Travel Wallet
  • Travel Log
Invest apps
  • Switchwon
  • Korean Investment & Securities
Key insights
01
Weakness
All competitors used different buy and sell prices and buried the fee, which drove comparison fatigue.
02
FX-first apps showed rate trends while travel apps hid them.
03
Every sign-up flow was long and jargon-heavy.
Key takeaway
  • Competitor research showed us exactly where users' pain points come from.
  • By targeting competitors' weaknesses, we positioned our value around our differentiators.
→ Next step: hold a workshop to share the findings gathered so far and align by answering the questions raised earlier.
Value-prop Workshop

With research insights in hand, I held a workshop to answer the Unknowns.
The insights became our decision-making criteria, and here's what we landed on.

1Who are our users?
Primary target : FX traders AND Toss Bank account holders
Why FX traders?
  • Our interviews showed they switch services readily for a better fee, so "no exchange fees" hits them hardest.
  • This segment also travels, so it naturally covers the traveler segment too.
  • Their account-transaction data showed 74.8% had invest-related activity and 59.1% travel-related, so targeting FX traders should raise the open rate.
Why Toss Bank account holders?
  • To open a multi-currency account, users first need a Toss Bank account, the KRW deposit account.
  • Toss Bank already has 15M users who can open an FX account with near-instant verification, so nudging them to open could lift the open rate.

Product strategy
"No exchange fees, forever"
After the workshop, the PO aligned departments and leadership around a single buy/sell price to remove the fatigue of hidden fees. This promise became our product strategy.
Key takeaway

  • The whole team aligned, and each member defined their own next actions.

6Promotion Collaboration

I reached out to the Exchange Rate Calculator team, as well as the Rate Alert, Travel section, and Toss Bank Card team. After discussing potential collaboration points using design mockups, we began surfacing the Toss Bank multi currency account in those placements. I also created several banner and push notification variants (such as the ad banner shown after a transfer and the top banner on the Toss app) based on user insights, then ran A/B tests.

Validating the design challenge
01Surface "no fee" clearly, everywhere it matters.Validated
After launch, "no fee" made a big splash. "No Exchange Fees Forever" posts and screenshots went viral across blogs, YouTube, and social media. Roughly 800 pieces of content appeared in a single month.
02Aggressively drive sign-up from existing Toss Bank users.Validated
88% were existing users and 12% were new to Toss Bank, the numbers show the strategy worked.
03Show the rate trend: today's rate and today's change.Partially validated
Since this info shows up wherever the rate appears, I couldn't isolate whether it directly drove exchanges. But a month after account opening, over 43% of users had exchanged, and on down-market days the share of sells ran about 20% above average, so I judged it had some effect.
04Keep the UX simple and the language plain.Validated
After launch, I ran a light survey: 82% of users said they had no trouble understanding how to use the service.
8Next Step

The Savings tab sees 3M visitors a day, making it the highest-traffic surface for the FX account. One week after launch, this page drove a 9.75% CTR and 0.65% CVR, low compared to our other products, so to improve it I translated additional findings from user research into several variants and validated them through A/B testing.
